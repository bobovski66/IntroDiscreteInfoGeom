Entropy and its discrete derivatives

Investigation 5.1

On a four-token binary lattice, calculate the entropy at \(k=0,1,2,3,4\) using natural logarithms.

  1. Convert \(k\) to a probability \(k/4\).

  2. Show the five entropy values to four decimals.

  3. Explain the symmetry of the table.

Investigation 5.2

Using your entropy table,

  1. compute all four first differences,

  2. compute all three second differences,

  3. identify the sign change of the first differences, and

  4. interpret that change as a maximum.

Investigation 5.3

Define a rule that moves one token to an adjacent binary state with larger entropy, stopping when no neighbor is better.

  1. Start at \(k=0\) and list all visited states.

  2. Record entropy changes.

  3. Verify that every listed change is nonnegative.

Investigation 5.4

Choose a probability vector \(p\) with three categories and four tokens.

  1. Calculate its entropy.

  2. Find all legal neighboring states.

  3. Calculate their entropy values.

  4. Determine whether \(p\) is a local maximum on the lattice.

Investigation 5.5

Take the trajectories of an entropy-increasing rule and an unequal-multiplier growth rule.

  1. Calculate three consecutive entropy values for each.

  2. Compare signs of \(\Delta H\).

  3. Explain why entropy production is a statement about the update law, not probability in general.
