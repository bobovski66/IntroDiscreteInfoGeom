Investigation 1.1

Start with the sequence \(a_n=n^2\) for \(n=0,1,2,3,4,5\).

  1. Calculate the six values.

  2. List every first difference \(\Delta a_n\).

  3. Calculate the second differences.

  4. Explain what constant second differences tell you.

Investigation 1.2

Suppose the daily number of new messages is \(b_n=3n+2\), beginning at day \(n=0\).

  1. Calculate \(b_0,b_1,b_2,b_3\).

  2. Compute the running totals \(S_0,S_1,S_2,S_3,S_4\), where \(S_0=0\).

  3. Verify \(\Delta S_2=b_2\).

Investigation 1.3

Let \(A_0=20\), \(B_0=60\), \(w_A=2\), and \(w_B=1\).

  1. Compute counts at steps 0, 1, and 2.

  2. Calculate the A proportions \(x_0,x_1,x_2\).

  3. Find \(\Delta x_0\) and \(\Delta x_1\).

  4. Verify the telescoping identity \(\Delta x_0+\Delta x_1=x_2-x_0\).

Investigation 1.4

In the original growth demonstration, start at proportion \(x_0=0.2\) with relative multiplier \(2\).

  1. Record five consecutive proportions.

  2. Subtract to construct their difference sequence.

  3. Add those differences and compare with the net change.

  4. Explain why the differences get smaller near the limiting state.

Investigation 1.5

Choose one sequence of your own whose first differences are constant and another whose second differences are constant.

  1. Write five terms of each.

  2. Show the appropriate difference tables.

  3. Explain how the original sequence can be recovered by summing changes.
