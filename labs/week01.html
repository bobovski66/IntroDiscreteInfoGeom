Investigation 1.1

Start with counts \((20,80)\) and multipliers \((2,1)\). Make a table for steps zero through six, recording both counts, the total, the A proportion, and the odds. Explain why the odds column is simpler than the proportion column.

Investigation 1.2

In the demonstration, set the initial A proportion to \(0.20\) and the relative multiplier to \(2\). Compare the first six computed proportions with your table. Use the exact formula to determine the first step at which A has proportion at least \(0.95\).

Investigation 1.3

Choose multipliers for which both types grow in count while A falls in proportion. Calculate three steps and explain the role of the total. Compare your result with a second initial population ten times as large.

Investigation 1.4

Use \(p=(1/2,1/3,1/6)\) and multipliers \((1,2,3)\). Compute two normalized updates. Show that multiplying every multiplier by seven changes neither updated table.

Investigation 1.5

Write a short algorithm for normalized growth. State how it checks nonnegative proportions, unit total, and positive multipliers. Explain why rounding to whole percentages at each step can alter the support of a trajectory.
