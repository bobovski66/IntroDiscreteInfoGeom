Discrete gradients on the probability simplex

Investigation 3.1

Take \(N=4\).

  1. List all 15 count triples \((a,b,c)\) adding to four.

  2. Draw their triangular arrangement or use the lattice demo.

  3. Count neighbors of \((2,1,1)\) and \((4,0,0)\).

  4. Explain why the boundary has fewer neighbors.

Investigation 3.2

Starting at \((2,1,1)\), move one token from C to A and one from A to B, in separate trials.

  1. Record both new count triples.

  2. Convert the triples to probabilities.

  3. Calculate the entropy at each distribution, taking \(0\log0=0\).

  4. Determine the signs of both directional differences.

Investigation 3.3

At an interior state \(p\), define \(u=e_A-e_C\) and \(v=e_B-e_C\).

  1. Write \(p+u/N\) and \(p+v/N\).

  2. Explain when each transfer is legal.

  3. Evaluate the two differences of \(H\) at \(p=(1/4,1/4,1/2)\) with \(N=4\).

Investigation 3.4

Choose two different three-step routes on the token lattice with the same start and finish.

  1. Label the entropy at each visited point.

  2. Add the successive entropy differences along each route.

  3. Explain why the route sums agree, even if the intermediate values differ.

Investigation 3.5

Draw a triangle of three allowed states and assign a clockwise flow of one unit along each edge.

  1. Compute divergence at each vertex.

  2. Compute the circulation around the triangle.

  3. Explain how zero divergence can coexist with nonzero circulation.
