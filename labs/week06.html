Relative entropy and finite contraction

Record calculations, predictions, and observations. Answers are saved in this browser when storage is available; use the download button to keep a separate copy.

Investigation 6.1

For \(q=(1/2,1/2)\) and \(p=(3/4,1/4)\), compute both directed relative entropies. Explain the meaning of each argument and why the results need not agree.

Investigation 6.2

An independent binary sample has counts \((8,2)\). Compare the empirical and fair-model likelihoods directly and through \(10D(\widehat q\|p)\). Report both a log-likelihood ratio and a likelihood ratio.

Investigation 6.3

In the demonstration, choose target A proportion \(0.5\), initial A proportion \(0.9\), and mixture fraction \(0.5\). Record five steps of the table, the actual \(D(q\|p_t)\), and the bound \((1-\lambda)^tD(q\|p_0)\).

Investigation 6.4

Prove the coordinatewise arithmetic-geometric inequality used in the contraction argument. Explain what happens if the initial table has a zero where the target is positive and why the first mixture step repairs that support mismatch.

Investigation 6.5

Choose two three-label tables and a mixture fraction. Verify the entropy concavity identity numerically, including both divergence terms. Explain what the identity says about hiding the source label when tables are pooled.
