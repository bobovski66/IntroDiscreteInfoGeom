Week 7 · Lab

Discrete Fisher geometry and statistical distance

Investigation 7.1

For binary distributions \(p=(1,0)\) and \(q=(1/4,3/4)\),

  1. calculate their square-root vectors on the radius-two circle,

  2. find the overlap \(\sum_i\sqrt{p_iq_i}\), and

  3. calculate the exact Fisher–Rao distance.

Investigation 7.2

Compare the moves \(x:0\to1/4\) and \(x:1/4\to1/2\).

  1. Write both coordinate changes.

  2. Compute the arc distances by \(2|\arcsin\sqrt y-\arcsin\sqrt x|\).

  3. Explain why equal coordinate steps have unequal lengths.

Investigation 7.3

Take \(p=(1/2,1/2)\) and \(q=(11/20,9/20)\).

  1. Calculate \(\delta=q-p\).

  2. Find \(g_p(\delta,\delta)=\sum_i\delta_i^2/p_i\).

  3. Calculate \(\sqrt{g_p(\delta,\delta)}\) and the exact spherical distance.

  4. Compare numerically.

Investigation 7.4

Take \(p=(1/4,3/4)\) and move to \(q=(1/2,1/2)\).

  1. Compute the local Fisher estimate at p.

  2. Compute the exact arc distance.

  3. Break the move into two equal probability steps and add their local estimates.

  4. Explain the effect of finer resolution.

Investigation 7.5

Consider an edge starting at \(p=(0,1)\).

  1. Explain why the expression \(\sum_i\delta_i^2/p_i\) is unavailable there for a move into the first category.

  2. Compute the exact Fisher–Rao distance from \((0,1)\) to \((1/4,3/4)\).

  3. Explain why the exact metric is useful.
